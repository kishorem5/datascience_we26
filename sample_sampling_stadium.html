What is Sampling? 🤔

Sampling is asking a smaller group of people (a sample) to learn about the whole crowd. For example, asking 500 fans about their favourite player instead of asking all 50,000.

Why sample?

It's faster, cheaper, and still gives a good idea of what the whole stadium thinks — as long as the sample is chosen carefully.

Probability Sampling — when randomness helps 🎲

Fair & measurable

Simple Random Sampling 🎟️

Randomly pick 500 ticket numbers from the 50,000 and ask those fans — everyone has an equal chance.

Systematic Sampling 🚶

Ask every 100th fan as they enter the stadium (1st, 101st, 201st ...). Easy and quick to implement.

Stratified Sampling 🧭

Divide fans by stands (North, South, East, West) and sample equally from each — ensures each section is represented.

Cluster Sampling 🪑

Pick a few seating blocks and survey everyone inside them — useful if it's hard to reach individual fans across the whole stadium.

Non-Probability Sampling — not random, but sometimes useful ✨

Quick & targetted

Convenience Sampling 🍔

Ask fans near the snack counters or the exits — fast but may be biased toward hungry or early-leaving fans.

Purposive Sampling 🌮

Intentionally ask fans with unusual snacks or outfits — useful when searching for a specific perspective.

Quota Sampling 👥

Decide to survey 200 men and 200 women to ensure a balanced count — not fully random, but meets targets.

Snowball Sampling 🔗

Ask one fan who then recommends another, and so on — helpful for reaching hidden groups but can cluster opinions.

Quick Tips for Beginners ✅

  • Think about who you can reach easily and who you might be missing.
  • Random methods help reduce bias — but practicality matters.
  • Mix methods if needed (e.g., stratified + random) to get the best balance.
